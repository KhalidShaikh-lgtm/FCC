Frontier vs Xfinity: Which Internet Provider Is Better?

Both Frontier and Xfinity sell home internet, but they are not always available at the same address. Here is how they compare on coverage, speed and price.

Availability: who can you actually get?

Of the 29,663 ZIP codes in our data, 756 have both Frontier and Xfinity reporting service, 3,252 have only Frontier, and 6,587 have only Xfinity. That means in many places you will only have one of the two, and the better choice is simply whichever one serves your address. Enter your ZIP on the home page to see which of them report service where you live.

Speed and connection type

Frontier is mostly fiber and DSL; Xfinity is mostly cable and fiber. Fiber usually gives the most consistent speeds and upload performance, cable is widely available and fast on download, and DSL or fixed wireless can be slower and vary more by address. In the data, Frontier's top reported speed is 7 Gbps and Xfinity's is 2 Gbps, but that is the best tier in the best place, not what every address gets.

Price

Frontier: from about $45/mo. Fiber 1 Gig for $64.99/mo with a 5-year price guarantee, no contracts, free Whole-Home Wi-Fi and installation. Xfinity: from $20/mo. Watch for what happens when the promotional price ends, equipment fees, data caps and whether you need AutoPay or a bundle to get the low price.

How to choose

  • If only one of them serves your address, that decides it. Check both pages: Frontier and Xfinity.
  • If both serve you, compare the total first-year cost including equipment and fees, not just the headline monthly price.
  • Pick the faster tier only if you need it. Most homes do well with 300 to 500 Mbps; see how much speed you need.
  • Ask for the price you will pay in month 13, and whether there is a contract or early termination fee.

Support numbers
