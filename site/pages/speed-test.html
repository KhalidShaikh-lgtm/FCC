How to read your results

Download

How fast data reaches your devices. It drives streaming, browsing and downloads. This is the number most plans advertise.

Upload

How fast you send data out. It matters for video calls, cloud backups and posting large files.

Ping (latency)

The delay in milliseconds before data starts moving. Under 30 ms feels instant; over 100 ms can hurt gaming and calls.

Jitter

How much your latency wobbles. Low jitter means smooth calls and games.

Get the most accurate result

  1. Connect a computer to the router with an Ethernet cable if you can.
  2. Pause streaming, downloads and cloud backups on other devices.
  3. Close extra tabs and VPNs, then run the test two or three times.
  4. Compare with the speed on your bill. Wi-Fi often shows 20 to 50 percent less than wired.
Slower than your plan? Restart your modem and router, move closer to the router, or follow our troubleshooting guides for your provider. Still slow? Compare providers at your address.

This test uses the Cloudflare speed test engine and measures the connection between your device and the nearest Cloudflare server. Results vary by device, Wi-Fi and time of day.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good internet speed?

For most homes, 100 Mbps download handles HD and 4K streaming, video calls and gaming for several people. Larger households and heavy uploaders benefit from 300 Mbps or more.

Why is my speed lower than my plan?

Wi-Fi interference, distance from the router, older devices, busy hours and other devices using bandwidth all lower results. Test with an Ethernet cable to see your true line speed.

Is this speed test free?

Yes. It is free, needs no sign-up, and nothing is stored except your last few results on your own device.

How often should I test?

Test when something feels slow, after changing plans or equipment, and at different times of day to spot peak-hour slowdowns.
