Availability: who can you actually get?

Of the 29,663 ZIP codes in our data, 22,139 have both T-Mobile and Verizon reporting service, 5,077 have only T-Mobile, and 549 have only Verizon. That means in many places you will only have one of the two, and the better choice is simply whichever one serves your address. Enter your ZIP on the home page to see which of them report service where you live.

Speed and connection type

T-Mobile is mostly 5G Home Internet and fiber; Verizon is mostly Fios fiber and 5G Home. Fiber usually gives the most consistent speeds and upload performance, cable is widely available and fast on download, and DSL or fixed wireless can be slower and vary more by address. In the data, T-Mobile's top reported speed is 2 Gbps and Verizon's is 2.3 Gbps, but that is the best tier in the best place, not what every address gets.

Price

T-Mobile: from $50/mo. From $30/mo with a voice line and AutoPay. Verizon: from $50/mo. 5G Home $50/mo with Auto Pay; $30-$35 with a Verizon mobile plan. Watch for what happens when the promotional price ends, equipment fees, data caps and whether you need AutoPay or a bundle to get the low price.

T-Mobile: Promo $30 with a qualifying voice line. 5G Home availability depends on your address. Verizon: Verizon prices need Auto Pay. Fios or 5G Home depends on your address.

How to choose

  • If only one of them serves your address, that decides it. Check both pages: T-Mobile and Verizon.
  • If both serve you, compare the total first-year cost including equipment and fees, not just the headline monthly price.
  • Pick the faster tier only if you need it. Most homes do well with 300 to 500 Mbps; see how much speed you need.
  • Ask for the price you will pay in month 13, and whether there is a contract or early termination fee.
