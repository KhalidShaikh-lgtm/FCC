Slow Internet: Causes and Fixes

Slow internet is usually a Wi-Fi, equipment or plan problem, not always your provider. Test first, then fix the weakest link.

Speed it up

  1. Run a speed test. Use our speed test, ideally on a computer connected by Ethernet, and compare the result to the speed you pay for.
  2. Test both wired and Wi-Fi. If wired is fast and Wi-Fi is slow, the problem is your router placement or Wi-Fi setup.
  3. Move the router. Put it in a central, open spot, off the floor and away from thick walls, microwaves and cordless phones.
  4. Use the 5 GHz band for devices close to the router. It is faster than 2.4 GHz but covers less distance.
  5. Pause heavy downloads and cloud backups, and check how many devices are streaming or gaming at the same time.
  6. Restart the modem and router to clear temporary problems.
  7. Update or replace old equipment. A router that is more than five years old may not handle current speeds.
  8. Compare your plan to your needs. A 100 Mbps plan can struggle with several 4K streams. See how much speed you need.
  9. Try different times of day. If it is only slow in the evening, congestion may be the cause, which your provider can look into.

Speed test results: what they mean

  • Under 25 Mbps download: fine for basic browsing and one video stream, tight for a family.
  • 25 to 100 Mbps: good for HD streaming and video calls for a few people.
  • 100 to 500 Mbps: comfortable for most households, including 4K and gaming.
  • 500 Mbps and up: useful for large households and heavy uploads.

If the result is far below your plan speed even on a wired connection, contact your provider. If a faster plan or a different provider is available, check your ZIP code.

Provider support numbers

If these steps do not fix it, call your provider using the number on your bill or on its own site. We list official numbers for the largest providers on our provider support page.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my internet slow only on Wi-Fi?

Distance, walls, interference and old routers slow Wi-Fi. Test with an Ethernet cable to confirm.

Is it normal to get less than the advertised speed?

Some difference is normal, especially on Wi-Fi, but wired tests should be close to your plan speed. Large gaps are worth raising with your provider.

Will a new router make my internet faster?

It can, if the router is the bottleneck. It cannot make your connection faster than your plan allows.

What internet speed do I need?

Most households do well with 300 to 500 Mbps. See our guide for a breakdown by household size and use.
