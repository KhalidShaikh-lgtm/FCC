Availability: who can you actually get?

Of the 29,663 ZIP codes in our data, 474 have both Xfinity and Spectrum reporting service, 6,869 have only Xfinity, and 8,191 have only Spectrum. That means in many places you will only have one of the two, and the better choice is simply whichever one serves your address. Enter your ZIP on the home page to see which of them report service where you live.

Speed and connection type

Xfinity is mostly cable and fiber; Spectrum is mostly cable and fiber. Fiber usually gives the most consistent speeds and upload performance, cable is widely available and fast on download, and DSL or fixed wireless can be slower and vary more by address. In the data, Xfinity's top reported speed is 2 Gbps and Spectrum's is 1 Gbps, but that is the best tier in the best place, not what every address gets.

Price

Xfinity: from $20/mo. Spectrum: from $30/mo. 300 Mbps for $30/mo with Auto Pay, plus free Advanced WiFi and a free mobile line for 1 year. Watch for what happens when the promotional price ends, equipment fees, data caps and whether you need AutoPay or a bundle to get the low price.

How to choose

  • If only one of them serves your address, that decides it. Check both pages: Xfinity and Spectrum.
  • If both serve you, compare the total first-year cost including equipment and fees, not just the headline monthly price.
  • Pick the faster tier only if you need it. Most homes do well with 300 to 500 Mbps; see how much speed you need.
  • Ask for the price you will pay in month 13, and whether there is a contract or early termination fee.

Support numbers

Both providers list support numbers on their own sites; we collect them on each provider page: Xfinity support and Spectrum support.

Sometimes. 474 ZIP codes in our data have both reporting service, but availability is by address, so check each provider.
