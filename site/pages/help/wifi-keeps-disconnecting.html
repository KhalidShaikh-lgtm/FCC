Wi-Fi Keeps Disconnecting

If Wi-Fi drops every few minutes, the cause is usually interference, distance or settings, and you can often fix it yourself.

Fix the drops

  1. Check if all devices drop. If only one does, update or reset that device's network settings. If all drop, the router is the likely cause.
  2. Restart the router and the modem.
  3. Move the router away from walls, microwaves, baby monitors and metal objects, and place it higher in a central room.
  4. Switch bands. Use 5 GHz for nearby devices and 2.4 GHz for far ones. Giving each band a separate name can make this easier.
  5. Change the channel. Crowded neighborhoods can cause interference. Set the router to auto, or try channels 1, 6 or 11 on 2.4 GHz.
  6. Update the router firmware in its app or admin page.
  7. Reduce the load. Many devices on one old router can cause drops.
  8. Add coverage. A mesh system or a wired access point works better than a basic extender in larger homes.
  9. Replace aging equipment if the router is several years old or runs hot.

If the connection also drops on a device connected by Ethernet, the problem is the line or modem, not Wi-Fi. See internet not working.

Provider support numbers

If these steps do not fix it, call your provider using the number on your bill or on its own site. We list official numbers for the largest providers on our provider support page.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my Wi-Fi disconnect every few minutes?

Common causes are interference, a weak signal at that spot, an overloaded or old router, or a device that keeps switching networks.

Do Wi-Fi extenders help?

They can, but they often cut speed in half. A mesh system or a wired access point is usually better for larger homes.

Should I call my internet provider?

Yes, if wired devices also drop or the modem lights show errors. Otherwise start with the router settings.

Can a VPN cause disconnections?

Sometimes. Turn it off briefly to see if the drops stop.
